L3 — the full document, progressively revealed

At L3 the deck stops being slides and becomes the complete document. Where L0–L2 are the fixed 1600×900 canvas scaled to fit, L3 reflows: the measure is capped at 72 characters, the layout responds to container width, and at ultra widths the body splits into two columns with a sticky table of contents.

This is the one place the kit deliberately abandons the scale-to-fit model. A long read must wrap to the reader's window, not shrink to a postcard. Container queries drive it, so the same article reflows correctly whether it sits in a narrow drawer or a 2560px ultra-wide.

Why L3 reflows instead of scaling

Scaling keeps a slide's composition intact — every element holds its relative place, which is exactly what you want for a designed slide. Prose is different: its composition is the wrap. Force prose onto a fixed canvas and you either overflow the frame or scale the type below legibility. Reflow keeps type at its designed size and lets the column breathe.

Measure cap

Body paragraphs and list items are capped at 72ch regardless of container width — the readability floor from the kit spec. Widen the window past the cap and the column stops growing; the gutter absorbs the rest.

Ultra two-column

Past 2000px of container width, the article body flows into two columns with headings spanning both. Switch the width tier to ultra 2560 above and widen your window to see it engage. The sticky TOC stays pinned on the left.

Provenance

Every claim in a real L3 document carries a source link back to the memory system it was mined from — the deck is generated from durable context, never invented at render time.

The deck is generated from the brain; L3 is the brain, laid out to read.
